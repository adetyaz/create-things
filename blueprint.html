Project blueprint · v2 · updated 31 October 2026

Pick one thing. Start small. Keep going when plans change.

To-do lists are passive: you write one, nothing pushes you, and the first time something takes over that time the plan falls apart. createthings is an active partner. It helps you pick one thing, always shows the next step (even what to search for and which tool to use), reshuffles your plan when life gets in the way, fits in new ideas without derailing you, keeps what you learn, and gives you a 3D pet that remembers your plans and depends on you showing up.

  1. Too many options. People save things faster than they act on them. The pile turns into guilt.
  2. No idea where to go next. They don't know the next step, which tool, or even what to search for.
  3. Nothing pushes them. A to-do list never tells you to do it.
  4. Plans break. Something takes the time, reshuffling by hand feels like starting over, so they give up.
  5. Learning doesn't stick. It fades in weeks, so every attempt starts from zero.

Who it's for: anyone scattered: designers, developers, writers, students, hobbyists, language learners. The AI adapts to what was saved, not to a profession.

🐾 The pet runs alongside the whole loop: it pushes you toward the next step, holds you to your plan, and eats when you do the work. Neglect it and it gets hungry, runs away, then dies.

Principles every feature is judged by

  1. Fewer choices, not more. One thing at a time.
  2. Starting beats planning. A first action within a minute.
  3. There is always a next step. Never "now figure it out".
  4. Plans bend, they don't break. Reshuffle, never restart.
  5. Never miss twice. After a miss, the 2-minute version comes first. Atomic Habits
  6. Obvious, easy, satisfying. Set time and place, tiny start, instant reward. Atomic Habits
  7. Protect focus. Time blocks, rituals, park interruptions. Deep Work
  8. Letting go counts as progress.
  9. Limit what's in progress. 1–3 Focus items (default 1).
  10. Learning should compound. It comes back until it sticks.
  11. Identity over outcomes. "You design every morning now."
  12. No guilt, but real consequences. Always a free way back.
  13. Showing up beats skipping. Two minutes always counts.
  14. The interface shows how you've been doing.
  15. You can't buy your way out of showing up.
02 · Momentum

The heart of it: push, reshuffle, always a next step

Plan + reshuffle

  • Steps go into time blocks: day + time + place, or a habit anchor ("after coffee")
  • The pet nudges at the block's time
  • Missed a block: offered a new slot, never moved silently; today is rebuilt
  • Never miss twice: the next day opens with the 2-minute version
  • Busy week: "Only 2 blocks free? Here's what moves."
  • New ideas go through fit-it-in: add, swap, park, or a 5-minute note

Always know the next step

  • One clear next step, 15–30 minutes
  • A 2-minute version of every step
  • What to search for: real search terms
  • Tools and resources per step
  • Finish a topic and the roadmap re-plans what's next from what you know now

Help me choose

  • 2–4 options side by side: what each gives for your goal, price, learning curve, trade-offs
  • Learning: free and beginner-friendly first. Building: fastest to a real result
  • Live web search with sources and "checked on" dates
  • Always ends with one pick, added to the plan and remembered

Rituals Deep Work

Start: every block starts the same way: sounds on, the pet sleeps, interruptions parked. Daily shutdown: a 1-minute wrap-up with the pet: what got done, what moves, what's first tomorrow.

Measured by showing up

The weekly digest leads with focused hours and sessions (the lead measure), then blocks kept and reshuffled, written as identity: "12 sessions: you're someone who designs now."

05 · The pet

An accountability partner that eats your work

Species at launch

Dog, cat, fluffy monster, dragon, each with breeds. Bears are the first drop after launch. Seasonal limited editions are bought with credits. Trend pets are original designs, never copies of famous characters.

Look and feel

3D, fluffy to badass sliders, designed in Spline. Walks around every screen, has a room on Today, idles, sleeps, purrs, eats, gets sad. Lightweight fallback on low-power phones and the extension.

Personality

Set by species and breed (eager dog, dramatic husky, sarcastic cat, proud dragon) with a gentle-to-savage tone slider. It changes how the pet talks, never what it advises.

Hunger, running away and death default timeline · user picks theirs at onboarding

0–2 days

Happy

Lively, clean app.

3–6

Peckish

Dust appears.

7–13

Hungry

Cobwebs, scattered food scraps.

14–29

Starving

Heavy dust, empty bowl. One feed brings it back up.

30–59

Ran away

Empty bed, pawprints. 3 days of activity bring it home.

60+

Died

Skeleton, then a memorial. Revive with credits or earn an egg in ~7 days.

Minimums: runs away no sooner than 10 days, dies no sooner than 21. More lenient any time, stricter once a month. Rest mode pauses the clock. Hunger is calculated from the last feed, not updated by a job.

Customization

Body sliders are free. Accessories (head, face, neck, back, held item) are earned at growth milestones or bought. Shared attachment points fit every species. Purely cosmetic; items belong to your account.

Living interface

Dust, cobwebs, bones, then an empty bed and a skeleton follow the pet's state. Your first feed after a gap sweeps it clean. Weather, day/night and seasons match your city (purely cosmetic).

Micro-interactions

Looks at your cursor, purrs on click, reads beside your journal, curls up for pomodoros, catches food when a step completes, runs to you when you come back. Liveliness: calm / normal / chaotic.

Memory: write

  • Session note, plan change, skipped pick
  • AI writes a one-line summary
  • memories row queued in Postgres
  • Queue paced to relayer limits (remember = 5 pts)
  • Saved on Walrus mainnet

Memory: read

  • Pick for me, roadmap, stuck, fit-it-in, chat
  • Walrus recall (1 pt)
  • Added to the prompt
  • Pet answers and says which memory it used

Payment

  • Pro request without credits
  • 402 with an MPP challenge
  • Wallet pays USDC on Sui
  • Tatum Sui gRPC confirms: success, recipient, amount, not reused, signer
  • Ledger row, then the answer

Rules that let it scale

  1. Hunger is calculated from the last feed, never updated in bulk.
  2. Every AI call: credits/quota check → queue → AI Gateway, capped per user.
  3. Weather cached per city for ~1 hour; cost grows with cities, not users.
  4. Spikes queue instead of crashing.
  5. Pet models and sounds served from R2 (no egress fees) and cached by the PWA.

    Their own Sui wallet in the browser, or a server-held agent wallet for the demo? @suimpp/mpp expects a signing key, so a browser wallet needs its custom-execute hook plus wallet message signing.

    Who funds the mainnet test wallet (some USDC + SUI for gas), and which address is SUI_PAYMENTS_ADDRESS?

    Help me choose needs live prices with sources. Which search API (cost, quality, and whether results can be shown with sources)?

    Needs knowledge review (Phase 5), outside the demo scope. Bring review in, or leave that type for later?

    Build the first species in Spline yourself, or start one with the Spline tools here?
